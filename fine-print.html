Fine Print

Does the proportionality of administrative fines shape citizens' compliance behaviour in Kazakhstan, 2019–2024?

PositivistAdministrative finesKazakhstan
The puzzle

A fine is meant to correct. Some call it "Finestan."

Lawmakers, a 2018 reform, and a 2026 amnesty all point to the same question: has the fine system drifted from deterrence into revenue?

01 · Research question

Does fine proportionality affect citizens' compliance behaviour?

Who: fined citizens & SMEsWhere: KazakhstanWhen: 2019–2024
02 · Where I stand

Positivist

Ontology

Compliance is a behaviour that happened or didn't — payment, appeal, repeat offence. It can be counted.

Epistemology

A fine-size effect on compliance is testable against registry data, not just described.

Cause and effect, not meaning — so measurement, not interpretation, answers it.

03 · Hypothesis

Disproportion breaks trust, and trust drives compliance

When a fine feels disproportionate to the offence, people read it as unfair rather than corrective — and unfair rules get avoided, not obeyed. (Tyler, 1990)

04 · What others found

Two traditions, one domestic debate

Theory

Procedural justice

People obey rules they see as fair — legitimacy, not fear, drives compliance. Tyler (1990)

Theory

Deterrence

Penalties work when proportionate, certain, and swift. Beccaria (1764)

Kazakhstan

"Finestan" critique

A Majilis deputy argues fines should solve problems, not fund budgets — evidence proportionality is contested here.

Kazakhstan

2018 reform

Fines cut ~30% across 150+ Code articles, easing pressure on business — the state already treats size as the problem.

Kazakhstan

Institutional trust

Perceived corruption lowers institutional trust nationally — a plausible channel from "unfair fine" to "won't pay." Makulbayeva et al. (2025)

05 · Scope

Empirical, bounded, answerable

EmpiricalAsks whether disproportionality changes behaviour — not whether it should.
PopulationKazakhstani citizens and SMEs fined under the Administrative Code.
Time2019–2024 — the post-reform, pre-amnesty window.
DataPayment, appeal, and repeat-offence rates from state registries and Atameken reporting.
06 · What I expect — and could be wrong

Expectations

  • 1Non-payment rises with fine-to-income ratio, not fine size alone.
  • 2Repeat offences cluster around penalties seen as arbitrary.
  • 3The 2018 reform helped, but "Finestan" complaints suggest it wasn't enough.
